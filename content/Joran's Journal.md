## Session 0

*Session notes: [[Session 0]]*

The Month of May   The feast tonight stretched longer than any man had the patience for, save perhaps [[Celia Bruce-Arden|Lady Celia]], who somehow manages to entertain the tedium with grace. [[Godric Arden|Godric]], however, has no such endurance, and I cannot fault him for it. As the sun dipped below [[The Green Isles|the Green Isles]]’ horizon, he whispered to [[Celia Bruce-Arden|Lady Celia]] who graciously dismissed the lords and ladies of the court.. The relief on [[Godric Arden|Lord Godric]]’s face was plain enough, though he hid it well from the nobles. A ruler's burdens seem endless, and his gaze grows heavier each day.

After my shift, I found [[Olivia Arden|Olivia]] in her chambers, quietly working through lessons no I only wish I understood. She reminds me of the life I might have had, had my people not twisted it. Perhaps it is why I care for her as if she were my own flesh and blood. I gave her the carving of a [[Olivia's Lioness Carving|lioness]] I had been whittling. She lit up, her smile as bright as any sunrise. Her joy inspires my own. [[Sir Willis Braxton|Sir Braxton]], guarding her door, approved of the gift with a knowing nod and a word of thanks.

Later, I crossed paths with [[Elara]]. She carries herself with a grace I’ll never understand, her movements like a melody. The [[Celia Bruce-Arden|Lady Celia]] keeps her as a bard, though [[Elara]] seems more inclined to stir hearts with her drama than calm them. Yet, her presence brings life to these halls, and for that, I cannot fault her.

Tonight, sleep brought no peace.

The dream was vivid, dark, and unlike any I have ever known. A city burned on a distant hill, its fires consuming the world around it. Two corpses—a stag and a lion—lay in a barren field. Between them, a broken crown and sword rotted, their edges dulled by time. [[Elara]] was there, and though I could not approach the dark figure watching the city burn, we spoke of the terror before us. The figure raised a flame, unquenchable and full of destruction. I awoke to a new dawn, but the image lingers like smoke in my mind.

***

[[Elara]] sought me out this morning. She, too, shared the same dream. I asked the cleric who tutors [[Olivia Arden|Olivia]] about the figure, but he had no answers. [[Olivia Arden|Olivia]] overheard, her concern masked by her usual composure. She is sharp beyond her years, though I wish she did not need to be.

[[Elara]] and I agreed: [[Godric Arden|Lord Godric]] must know. It is not a matter for silence. After ensuring [[Olivia Arden|Olivia]] was safely handed off to another guard, I approached the study. [[Godric Arden|Godric]] allowed me to speak, though his annoyance was evident. The stag, he said, was [[House Grey]]. That much is clear, though the rest remains shrouded.

The arrival of riders interrupted the day. Bearing the sigil of a white wolf, they brought grave news: [[Godric Arden|Godric]]’s elder brother, [[Godfrey Arden|Godfrey]], is dead. A letter from the royal court confirmed the rumors of [[William Arden|William]]'s bastardy, and [[Godric Arden|Godric]] claimed the throne for himself. War is no longer a distant threat but a certainty. I came to these lands to escape the violence of the North. Yet here I stand, once again on the edge of bloodshed.

[[Godric Arden|Godric]]’s plan is bold—perhaps too bold. We are to infiltrate [[Brightguard]] Castle with two others, [[Thomas]] and [[Gerrit]], while his forces march on its walls. The risks are many, and success is far from assured.

As I write, the halls are alive with preparations for war. My heart is heavy with this news, burdened by the knowledge that we march toward fire, steel, and ruin. Still, I press on. For [[Olivia Arden|Olivia]], for the future she deserves, and for a peace I pray to see before my days are done.

PART 2

The sea carried us steadily southward, our ships cutting through the waves like blades. [[Godric Arden|Godric]]’s army filled the decks, their voices subdued, save for the occasional laugh or murmured prayer. [[Godric Arden|Godric]] himself sought us out—[[Elara]] and me—his curiosity about the dream still lingering. We had no new insight, only the image of the burning stag and the distant, ominous figure. I could see the frustration in his eyes, but he said nothing of it.

Instead, he shared memories of the last war—when his castle was besieged, and survival seemed impossible. He spoke not with glory but with the weight of a man who knows war’s cost. It was then I told him what had been on my mind: law and order, for all their flaws, can bring an end to senseless death. I am less troubled by the side I have chosen. [[Godric Arden|Godric]]’s cause is just, even if the path is bloodied. Still, I resolved to kill only when necessary. [[Elara]], ever pragmatic, agreed.

We reached landfall under the cover of darkness. Our landing was near a cave beneath [[Brightguard]] Castle—a hidden path known to our guides, [[Thomas]] and [[Gerrit]]. Both veterans of the last war, they had pledged themselves to [[Godric Arden|Godric]]’s cause, and their knowledge would prove invaluable. Armed and ready, we descended into the cave, the flickering torchlight casting long shadows on the walls.

The passage brought us into the castle’s lower levels, from which we climbed toward the surface. When we emerged near the courtyard, the scene was one of grim anticipation. Soldiers—roughly twenty—were scattered throughout. Some sat by a fire, others patrolled in pairs, and a few gathered at a guard post. The faint glow of torches illuminated the darkness, their light reflecting off steel.

We devised a plan. [[Thomas]] and [[Gerrit]] would create a distraction—a fire on the far side of the grounds—to draw the guards away. Then they would climb the ramparts to deal with the sentries above. [[Elara]] and I would handle the remaining men in the courtyard and open the gate to [[Godric Arden|Godric]]’s army. It had to be quick; failure meant death or worse.

The plan unfolded with precision. [[Thomas]] and [[Gerrit]] set their fire, the sudden flames drawing shouts and hurried footsteps as men rushed to contain the blaze. [[Elara]], ever the performer, stepped into the light, her voice and movements captivating the remaining guards. I circled unseen, and when the moment was right, she unleashed a Thunderwave that sent two of them sprawling. The rest fell quickly beneath my blade.

With the courtyard clear, we opened the gate. [[Godric Arden|Godric]]’s forces poured in, their chants of “[[Godric Arden|King Godric]]!” shaking the air. The defenders were overwhelmed, many captured, though a few met their end. Amid the chaos, my gaze fell upon a grisly sight: the body of a man, long dead, hanging in the courtyard. The stench was overwhelming, and [[Godric Arden|Godric]] demanded answers.

A captured guard, insolent yet terrified, claimed the man had been a thief who stole from [[William Arden|William]]. [[Godric Arden|Godric]]’s face darkened; this was not justice, and he made his displeasure known. The banners of [[House Lancaster]] were torn down and burned, replaced with [[Godric Arden|Godric]]’s. [[Brightguard]] Castle was his once more.

The days that followed were a flurry of activity. Repairs were made, stores replenished, and defenses strengthened. [[Godric Arden|Godric]]’s family arrived, bringing a moment of light amid the somber task of preparing for war.

Then came the rider, bearing news that sent ripples through the camp: [[Edward Grey|Edward Gray]] had been executed for treason. His son, [[Henry Grey|Henry Gray]], had declared himself king of an independent state and now marched to war against [[William Arden]] and [[House Lancaster]].

[[Godric Arden|Godric]] summoned [[Elara]] and me, his gaze hard as steel. He declared [[Henry Grey|Henry]] a traitor, though he saw an opportunity amid the chaos. The [[House Grey|Grays]] held [[Robert Lancaster]], heir to their house, as a prisoner. The [[House Lancaster|Lancasters]], in turn, had captured [[Sophia Grey|Sophia Gray]], [[Henry Grey|Henry]]’s sister.

Our task was clear: ride south, find [[Henry Grey|Henry Gray]], and bring him to heel. Either he would renounce his kingship and bend the knee or ally with [[Godric Arden|Godric]] against the [[House Lancaster|Lancasters]]. With war brewing on all sides, [[Godric Arden|Godric]] could not afford loose threads.

[[Elara]] and I accepted the mission. The road ahead is uncertain, but my resolve remains firm. I left the North to escape war, yet it seems fate has other plans. For now, I will walk this path, trusting that it may lead to a peace I have yet to see but still dare to hope for.


## Session 1

*Session notes: [[Session 1]]*

[[The Lowlands]] and [[Greentower]]

The gods have a dark humor, setting my feet on this road. Each step feels ordained by something wicked, as if the weight of my deeds has summoned this punishment.

The lowlands are vast and quiet, though even their beauty feels dulled under the shadow of my task. [[Godric Arden]] entrusted me with a sealed letter, its contents for [[Henry Grey|Henry Gray]] alone. The paper in my pack feels heavier than it should, and my thoughts turn to what might be asked of me should [[House Grey|Gray]] refuse to bend the knee.

[[Elara]] and I rode openly into a camp of [[House Grey|Gray]] soldiers. Four of them circled their campfire, their laughter fading as we approached. [[Elara]]’s charm softened their tension; she has a way of weaving light into dark places, though the shadows remain for me. I held the reins, silent, while she performed.

We told them our purpose, and though wary, they pointed us toward [[Greentower]]. Days south, they said, but their trust came at a cost—two gold. It was an easier price than blood.

That night, as [[Elara]] slept, I stared into the darkness. What is it that drives me forward? I carry no hope of redemption, only the grim certainty that this path is my due. Killing will follow me, as it always does.

The next day, we came across a homestead in ruin. The sight of bodies froze me in place. I have seen death in all its forms, yet the twisted tableau of this family—the man sobbing in their midst—was a cruelty that burned deeper than most. [[Elara]] healed him, though he begged for death. She is kinder than I, but sometimes kindness is not enough.

When he spoke of what had been done—[[House Lancaster|Lancaster]] men, raping and killing his wife and daughters—I felt the cold steel of rage slip between my ribs. The man’s grief was a mirror to my soul.

I seized him, pinned him to the wall of his shattered home, and told him to live, though it felt a hollow command. We buried the bodies together. It was a slow, agonizing task, the weight of each shovel full pressing harder on my spirit.

Tracking the ones responsible led us to a town where [[House Lancaster|Lancaster]] banners flew. I prayed the men hanging from the walls were guilty of that family’s suffering. A guard confirmed it was so—justice of some form, dealt by other hands. I thanked him and moved on. Still, the rage lingered, a restless beast gnawing at my chest.

[[Greentower]] rose before us, a vast encampment swelling with men and purpose. Fifty thousand strong, [[Henry Grey|Henry Gray]]’s forces stood as a monument to his defiance. The guards allowed us passage upon seeing [[Godric Arden|Godric]]’s seal, and we entered a world heavy with tension.

Another man, [[Raouen Sanderfell|Rowan Lawson]], entered with us. His easy demeanor was a balm against the severity of our mission. Yet beneath his words lay the quiet burden of a past he wished to keep hidden.

In [[House Grey|Gray]]’s chambers, I presented the letter, addressing him as “lord,” not “grace.” If he took offense, he did not show it. [[House Grey|Gray]]’s refusal to bend the knee was no surprise, though his willingness to entertain alliance gave me a flicker of hope. A letter, he said, would be sent to [[Godric Arden|Godric]], though whether words could stave off swords remained to be seen.

We met others in [[House Grey|Gray]]’s service—men bound to his cause. [[Theodore Silver]], whose sharp gaze seemed to pierce through to one’s soul. [[Draven Throssenheiser]], a half-elf with a restless edge. And [[Dharshoon]], whose presence was as steady as the earth beneath us.

Two others stood apart: [[Baylor Stelaris|Baylor]] and [[Sir Baylon Stelaris|Baylon Stelaris]]. Brothers, clad in the markings of [[Order of Holy Fire|the Order]] of the [[Order of Holy Fire|Holy Fire]], their presence a reminder of the old powers that still hold sway in [[The South|the South]]. [[Baylor Stelaris|Baylor]]’s crimson mask glinted in the firelight, and [[Sir Baylon Stelaris|Baylon]]’s greatsword, [[Starbinder]], was a thing of grim beauty.

Our next task pulls us back to [[Stoneheath]], where [[House Lancaster|Lancaster]] men hold sway. Convince them to leave, [[House Grey|Gray]] commands. It feels like asking wolves to abandon a fresh kill. Blood will likely spill again.

[[Theodore Silver|Theodore]]’s face darkened when I recounted the family’s slaughter. His silence was heavy, though whether it was anger or despair, I could not say. As for me, I will do what must be done, though each drop of blood will weigh on my soul. I deserve this burden. Perhaps I always have.

And so we ride.


## Session 2

*Session notes: [[Session 2]]*

[[Joran Jotunsson|Joran]]'s Journal – The Road to [[Stoneheath]]

Before Dawn
The gray stillness of early morning presses down on us as we prepare to leave. A [[The old trader|trader]], older than the hills he travels, sells us tokens said to hold some small magic. They are light in the palm but heavy with unspoken promises. [[Draven Throssenheiser|Draven]] haggles for spirits; the [[The old trader|trader]] wants too much. We pay for his words instead: a whispered warning of [[Stoneheath]]’s strength and its northern weakness.

The road leads us past the homestead again. The fields stand empty, save for the weight of memory. We dismount, and I find myself drawn to [[The farmer|the farmer]]’s home. A man is there, not [[The farmer|the farmer]] but a squatter. He has no answers, only a dagger that tells a soldier’s tale. I strike a bargain with him to keep the place until its rightful owner returns. It feels hollow, like much of what I do these days.

[[Theodore Silver|Theodore]] doesn’t trust him. His questions cut deeper than my grip, but in the end, we let the man go. His story is his own burden to bear, and mine is heavy enough.

The Encounter
[[Dharshoon]] spots torchlight on the road. We prepare, not knowing what will come. [[House Lancaster|Lancaster]] men arrive, six of them, weary and wary. They see [[Elara]] and me framed in the doorway. Their questions are sharp, but so are my answers.
When they notice my colors, their patience snaps like a frayed bowstring. Steel flashes in the dim light, and I am forced again to kill.

I move through the fight as if trapped in a waking dream, each swing of my axe a scream I cannot silence. Two fall quickly, their blood soaking into the dirt, while the others falter. [[Dharshoon]] spares one, but the mercy feels more cruel than kind. [[Theodore Silver|Theodore]]’s questions meet a wall of silence. Frustration rises.
I knock the man unconscious, hoping for a reprieve. But the others decide there is no choice. The prisoners must die, their silence a threat we cannot risk.
I cannot watch.

I step away, leaving the work to others. My hands are already too stained; I feel no need to deepen the red. A part of me wonders if this is what the dark god wills. To test my resolve, to weigh my soul in the blood of others.

The Aftermath
When it is done, the air is still again, save for the whispers of the dead. We bury them in [[The farmer|the farmer]]’s field, hiding their shame beneath the soil. [[Elara]] confronts the deserter, but he says nothing. She leaves, her anger soft as ash.

As we rest, I stare into the darkness. Each face I have killed rises in my mind, a procession of the damned. I feel their eyes upon me, judging. I hate this. I hate the killing.

And yet, I keep going.

Why?

Because I deserve it.

Whatever force harries me—be it some dark god or fate or something more malevolent—I accept its punishment. If this is my penance, so be it. I will endure, even as the weight of it threatens to break me.


## Session 3

*Session notes: [[Session 3]]*

The march to [[Stoneheath]] was steady, if uneasy. The old merchant’s words lingered in my mind—the hidden way into the town, our chance to strike unseen. We are not alone in this endeavor; [[Sir Baylon Stelaris|Baelon Stelaris]] and his men will follow behind us. Their steel is welcome, for we tread on uncertain ground.

Evening fell as we buried the [[House Lancaster|Lancaster]] men we slew. No time for prayers, no words for the dead, just dirt to silence the past. In the fading light, a lone rider approached—one of [[Sir Baylon Stelaris|Baelon]]’s men. The army would be in position by nightfall. A cloudy sky hung over us like a shroud, a gift from the heavens for our approach.

Through the woods we moved, shadows in the dark, until the sewer culvert revealed itself. It was filth, but filth would serve our purpose. [[Dharshoon]] handled the horses while [[Elara]] took watch. If we failed, she would run for the army. She did not like it, but she agreed.

[[Theodore Silver|Theodore]] led us in. The stink clung to us, made [[Draven Throssenheiser|Draven]] wretch, but we pressed on. The grate ahead shone with the dim light of torches beyond. I lifted [[Theodore Silver|Theodore]], [[Draven Throssenheiser|Draven]], and [[Raouen Sanderfell|Raouen]] through. [[Dharshoon]] and I remained in the dark, waiting. [[Dharshoon]] left to inform [[Elara]] of our progress while I stayed behind, listening to the dripping and the scuttling of vermin.

The three moved through the city in [[House Lancaster|Lancaster]] garb, ghosts among the living. They learned of the town’s masters—[[Sir James Stone|James]] and [[Sir Oliver Stone|Oliver Stone]], twin knights who ruled with an iron hand. [[Dharshoon]] returned and scouted ahead, finding other exits and a knight clad in plate above.

Then it all went wrong. [[Theodore Silver|Theodore]] and [[Raouen Sanderfell|Raouen]] were spotted. The Stone brothers confronted them, suspicion in their eyes. A lie was spoken, but not in unison. Steel was drawn. They were taken.

[[Draven Throssenheiser|Draven]] returned with the news. I sent him and [[Dharshoon]] back to [[Elara]]—to [[Sir Baylon Stelaris|Baelon]]. They must know what has happened. As they fled into the night, I remained, waiting, watching.

[[Sir Baylon Stelaris|Baelon]] arrived with 350 men. He moved swiftly, leading fifty into the sewers. We would strike from within while the rest attacked from without.

Inside the keep, the twins confronted [[Raouen Sanderfell|Raouen]]. They knew his lineage, knew the blood in his veins. They tossed him a sword and bade him fight. A duel of honor, or a cruel jest?

The moment came. We surged into the courtyard. I threw my javelin, drawing [[Sir James Stone|James Stone]]’s attention from [[Raouen Sanderfell|Raouen]]. [[Elara]] revealed her celestial form, a beacon in the chaos. [[Draven Throssenheiser|Draven]] cut a man down. I moved to shield [[Raouen Sanderfell|Raouen]], disarming [[Sir James Stone|James Stone]]. One by one, their men fell until only one remained, throwing down his arms in surrender.

Then [[Sir Baylon Stelaris|Baelon]] arrived, bloodied but unbowed. Victory was ours.

A [[House Lancaster|Lancaster]] knight in plate made one final, desperate stand. He charged [[Sir Baylon Stelaris|Baelon]] and was hewn in half by [[Starbinder]], the knight’s sword. Aeternium, a blade of legend. The steel of House [[House Stelaris|Stellaris]].

As the dust settled, I watched [[Sir James Stone]] crawl toward his fallen brother. He reached for him, but the strength left his body before he could touch him. I did not look away.

I wonder if I will die as he did, crawling toward something I cannot reach. The war does not end here. The path stretches onward, paved with blood and iron.

I do not know if I walk it willingly anymore.


## Session 4

*Session notes: [[Session 4]]*

[[Joran Jotunsson|Joran]]’s Journal — Entry after the Taking of [[Stoneheath]]

It is midnight. [[Stoneheath]] is ours. The weight of bloodshed hangs heavy in the cold night air, the scent of smoke and iron lingering on the breeze. I stand among the wounded and the dead, and though my heart aches for every life lost, I remind myself that we fought for order, not chaos. Sir [[Sir Baylon Stelaris|Baelon Stelaris]], stalwart in command, rewards us with coin — twenty-five gold pieces apiece — but wealth feels hollow after the screams of battle have faded.

We rest, but my dreams are troubled. Visions of the fallen — [[Sir James Stone|Sir James]], [[Sir Oliver Stone|Oliver Stone]] — brave men who died with honor. At dawn, [[Audrey Grey]] arrives, the Lady Regent in [[Lord Callum Douglas|Lord Douglas]]’ name. She forbids looting. Her decree pleases me. Even in war, there must be decency. I oversee the burial of the common soldiers, and for the knights, I send their bodies home with solemn ceremony. Over [[Sir James Stone|Sir James]] and young Oliver, I whisper an old Varanoran prayer, a hymn of guidance across the mists. I cannot say whether the old gods listen. But I believe they honor courage.

I break open my purse, buying splint armor. War is cruel, but I must endure it. [[Draven Throssenheiser|Draven]] gathers supplies for what lies ahead. [[Theodore Silver|Theodore]] speaks quietly with [[Sir Baylon Stelaris|Baelon]], and [[Dharshoon]] tends to his bow, trading beeswax for restoration. [[Elara]], ever playful, acquires a deck of cards — a small token of levity among shadows.

A man of [[House Stelaris|Stelaris]] approaches. We are summoned. [[Sir Baylon Stelaris|Baelon]] and another stand waiting — [[Baylor Stelaris|Bealor]], the red-masked enigma. They speak of disappearances. Women and men alike, vanished westward into the forest. This began before the war. My blood chills. Even the [[House Lancaster|Lancasters]] are missing men.

We follow their trail into the woods. [[Sir Baylon Stelaris|Baelon]] strides with us, but at the edge of shadow, he parts ways, leaving us alone beneath ancient boughs. The night is heavy with silence. Soon, a house emerges from the darkness, nestled into a cleft on a hill, footprints like scars in the damp earth. [[Elara]]’s mage hand opens the door; her magic card lights the room.

The stench of rot and soot clings to the air. Blood smears the walls, and scorch marks mar the wood. A closet hides horror — a father and his boy, lifeless. Their eyes burned away. A dark emblem carved into the father’s flesh; the boy’s chest hollow where his heart once beat. I cannot bear to enter. I stand outside, sentinel, while the others investigate. [[Draven Throssenheiser|Draven]] stumbles out, pale and trembling, clutching a blood-smeared doll. He shows it to me. Something breaks inside. My fists clench. I vow vengeance.

[[Elara]] finds signs of a mother and daughter. [[Dharshoon]] gently rescues a living queen bee from a dead hive. Even in desolation, life clings. A scream pierces the night. We run. Torchlight extinguished, we move swift and silent. [[Draven Throssenheiser|Draven]] leads. On a path, four [[House Lancaster|Lancaster]] soldiers lie broken, their heads impaled on pikes. The forest swallows mercy. We follow the trail up a hill. Beyond, in the valley, a [[The cult temple|temple]] complex looms, its altar slick with blood.

Figures step from shadow — twelve robed [[Cult of Abyssal Fire|cultists]] and a bound woman. They chant in tongues that poison the air. [[Raouen Sanderfell|Rouen]] fires an arrow, consumed by their conjured flames. They retreat, dragging the woman back into darkness. [[Elara]]’s visage becomes terrible and cold, cowing one [[Cult of Abyssal Fire|cultist]] with fear alone. The battle begins.

I do not hold back. The sight of that house, of the child — it awakens something savage within me. I tear through [[Cult of Abyssal Fire|cultists]] with abandon, no mercy, no hesitation. Their screams are fuel to my wrath. The part of me that recoils from killing is silent tonight. They are filth. They are evil made flesh.

As the last of them falls, he summons a demon — a towering beast encased in iron, wielding a spear of shadow. I clash with it, driving it back, forcing it to drop its weapon. It lifts me like a doll, squeezing my throat. I see the abyss. Then — holy fire. [[Baylor Stelaris|Baelor]] descends like judgment itself, cleaving the demon in two, saving my life.

[[Baylor Stelaris|Baelor]] enters the [[The cult temple|temple]], bringing forth the prisoner. [[Elara]] attempts to seize a ritual dagger, only to recoil from its malevolence. [[Baylor Stelaris|Baelor]] claims it, and with divine might, destroys it utterly. The [[The cult temple|temple]] crumbles at his command. I behead the [[Cult of Abyssal Fire|cultists]]’ corpses, preparing to burn them. But [[Baylor Stelaris|Baelor]]’s magic consumes them before I can strike flint. He turns to us, gratitude shining in his eyes. His [[Order of Holy Fire]] owes us a debt, he says.

We return to [[Stoneheath]] with the rescued woman, reuniting her with her kin. [[Baylor Stelaris|Baelor]], true to his word, bestows upon each of us a gift. I accept mine in silence, though my mind still roils. I find myself deeply intrigued by this [[Order of Holy Fire]]. Their purpose, their clarity — they remind me of the vows I took, the ideals I swore to uphold. Perhaps, in their fire, I might find something that aligns with the law and justice I serve.

Yet, the summoning of that demon disturbs me deeply. It is too familiar — dark rituals and forbidden rites, not unlike those whispered of in the frozen northlands of my birth. The same black magic that stains the honor of clans, the same evil that preys on weakness.

Tonight, I reflect on what true evil looks like, and remember the reason I still fight. I pray I never lose sight of who I am.


## Session 5

*Session notes: [[Session 5]]*

We ride still under [[Sir Baylon Stelaris|Baelon Stelaris]]’s banner. With me: [[Elara]], [[Raouen Sanderfell|Raouen]], [[Theodore Silver|Theodore]], [[Draven Throssenheiser|Draven]], and [[Dharshoon]], though the beekeeper remained behind this time to tend his hives. We’ve grown closer through battle, hardship, and quiet campfire talks. Strange company I never imagined I'd call brothers and sisters-in-arms.

This morning, I woke to find a gift: a pair of fine [[Bracers of the Holy Order|bracers]] left by [[Order of Holy Fire|the Order]] of [[Order of Holy Fire|Holy Fire]]—[[Baylor Stelaris|Baelor Stelaris]]’s order. An unspoken token of respect, perhaps for our part in breaking the [[Cult of Abyssal Fire|cult]]’s hold in the forest. I wore them with quiet reverence. I found [[Baylor Stelaris|Baelor]] before he rode out. He was already saddling up—his eyes fixed on some distant purpose. I asked about joining [[Order of Holy Fire|the Order]]. He didn't say no, but offered only a promise to speak more of it later. The way he gripped his reins, I don’t think he’s certain where his path ends.

We rode with [[Audrey Grey|Lady Audrey Grey]] to parley with [[Lord Callum Douglas]], a man of fading age and thin patience. His banner—red fox on green—met ours along a bend of the wooded road, where fog clung low over the hollows like a burial shroud. He looked at us—at me—with quiet disdain, the kind that sinks its teeth deeper than open hatred. [[Elara]] and he shared a cold exchange; some past wound lies between them, though she did not speak of it.

[[Lord Callum Douglas|Douglas]] tasked us with liberating a [[The hamlet|hamlet]] under siege. [[Raouen Sanderfell|Raouen]] challenged him—young, bold, maybe foolish. [[Lord Callum Douglas|Douglas]] shut him down with cold precision. [[Sir Baylon Stelaris|Baelon]] offered only a brief rebuke. I caught the steel in his tone.

We passed into the lowlands—green hills giving way to thinning trees and black soil. The smoke came first, then the church tower, fire licking up from its steeple like the tongues of the Abyss. We saw villagers being forced into the square—helpless, herded like cattle. My stomach turned. I could feel the leash straining within me.

We chose direct action. No time for stealth. [[Elara]] and I charged down the hill. Arrows sang around us. As I closed in, I saw a man in the square—tall, broad, wrapped in northern furs despite the heat of flame. A mercenary. I knew him before he called my name: [[Ulf]]. My cousin.

## Session 29

*Session notes: [[Session 29]]*

We lay on our bellies atop a ridge and counted the fires of an army too large to count. More men than I saw gathered even at [[Kingsmont]], spread out below us like a second city grown overnight. [[Grimhold]] sat only a few days ahead of us. Getting past a host that size, quietly, in time for the warning to matter, felt like trying to outrun a flood on foot.
[[Theodore Silver|Theo]] wanted to hurt them before we left, and so, if I am honest, did I. We searched the camp with our eyes for anything we might burn or break from where we lay and found nothing worth the risk. He sent word through [[Raouen Sanderfell|Raouen]]'s stone to [[Brutus]] at [[Sunwatch]] instead, with orders to warn [[Stag's Rest]] besides. A [[03 The Jihads|jihad]] is coming. It is a small thing, a warning carried by stone and rider rather than steel, but I have learned that small things matter more than a man's pride likes to admit.

[[Hadron Ironwood|Hadron]] looked the host over with a soldier's eye and told us it was still more a gathering than a weapon — disorganized, not yet dangerous in the way it would soon become. That gave us room to work. We weighed slipping a man into their camp — [[Dharshoon]] could pass well enough, with his look and his tongue — or running down a supply train before it reached them. In the end [[Draven Throssenheiser|Draven]] and [[Theodore Silver|Theo]] settled us on something quieter still: ride ahead of the host and foul every well along its road. [[Theodore Silver|Theo]] has a poisoner's training I did not ask too many questions about, and [[Dharshoon]] knows which of this land's plants kill as surely as any blade. [[Sir Baylon Stelaris|Baylon]] paced before we set out, nerves plain on him for once. I put a hand on his shoulder and told him we had faced worse and still stood. It was not much comfort, but it was what I had.

I did not love the work itself. I have killed more men than I can rightly number, but always with a blade in hand and their eyes on mine. Poison does not ask who drinks. We told ourselves it was soldiers' water, a column's water, and most nights that was likely true. It was not true at one well, where a [[The shepherd|shepherd]] woke to find [[Dharshoon]] crouched over his water with poison already in hand. He warned the man not to drink — the only mercy he could offer — then did what we'd come to do, and ran. I think of that [[The shepherd|shepherd]]'s flock more than I'd like to.

We found worse than that, and could do nothing at all about it. A slaver's [[Bloody Knives slavers|caravan]] on the road, elven women only, bound east. [[Draven Throssenheiser|Draven]] went still at the sight of their markings — [[The Bloody Knives|the Bloody Knives]], the syndicate that raised him before we did. Crates among their wagons glowed faint green. Wildfire, if I know that stink. [[Hadron Ironwood|Hadron]] did the arithmetic plainly, the way he does: we would die, and they would still be in chains. We rode on. I have not forgiven myself for that, and I do not expect to soon.

The [[03 The Jihads|jihad]] found us before [[Grimhold]] did. Hooves on open ground, torches by the hundred closing fast, no cover but a stand of trees [[Dharshoon]] spotted from a climb. We ran through the night and most of a day for [[Grimhold]]'s walls, his briars tearing at the riders' legs wherever he could lay them. They caught us anyway, almost within sight of the gates, arrows finding [[Hadron Ironwood|Hadron]]'s horse, then [[Theodore Silver|Theo]]'s, then [[Raouen Sanderfell|Raouen]]'s, then young [[Godfrey the Squire|Godfrey]]'s. I gave mine to [[Hadron Ironwood|Hadron]] and took the shape my fathers' blood lends me when I ask it for speed instead of strength, and ran the rest of the way with [[Theodore Silver|Theo]] over one shoulder and [[Godfrey the Squire|Godfrey]] over the other, faster than any horse still living behind us.

[[Grimhold]]'s horn saved what my legs could not have. Fifty knights crested the hill in a wedge, split clean around us without breaking stride, and crashed into the charge like a wave against stone. Only one of them fell buying us that. One, against a force meant to run us down entirely. I will carry that trade a long while.

[[Torren Grimston]] led them himself, and I have not been so glad to see a scarred, one-eyed face in some time. He'd had [[Theodore Silver|Theo]]'s warning days before we arrived — the only reason fifty men were saddled and ready instead of asleep. He took us in, gave us what water and news he had in place of wine, and told us [[Henry Grey|King Henry Grey]] has roused his banners and marched south, the [[House Douglas|Douglases]] having struck at some of [[Godric Arden|Godric]]'s own men while we were away. I said nothing aloud, but felt something in me settle hearing it. Whatever shape this war takes, I know still whose banner I ride under.

No smith at [[Grimhold]] could mend [[The Grand Marshal|the Grand Marshal]]'s blade or make sense of [[The Iron Fist|the Iron Fist]]'s ring, so I carry both a while longer. [[Hadron Ironwood|Hadron]] turned the ring over himself before we rode on — a cruel little thing, he said, good for an extra wound dealt in anger, paid for in the voices of every man you've killed that day, should your will slip. I have enough voices of my own some nights without borrowing more. I gave it to him gladly and did not look back at it.

We are bound now for a town called [[Sanctuary]], a fortnight north, summoned by [[Henry Grey|King Grey]] himself. Two months gone from [[Sunwatch]] already, and spring nearly spent around us without my marking when.

We broke the journey at [[Sunwatch]]. [[Brutus]] had the roofs sealed at last and the armory widened, four men under arms already and apologizing that it wasn't more. [[Theodore Silver|Theo]] only thanked him and asked for twelve instead. It did my heart good, seeing that castle mend itself a little more each time I lay eyes on it — proof that something in this war can still be built instead of only broken.

While [[Theodore Silver|Theo]] stayed to tend his people, the rest of us stepped through the [[The Everwhite Wardrobe|wardrobe]] once more into that old bitter cold, hoping [[Leana]] might know how to carry our quest forward now that a [[Handmaidens of Seraphyne|handmaiden]] lies dead and the [[The Kilns|kiln]] [[Hadron Ironwood|Hadron]] carries sits dark and lightless. He stood mesmerized at the threshold by the blood the door demands before it opens — a wizard's curiosity I understand but do not share. [[Artorius]]'s old camp stood empty, though someone had kept a fire there not long past, and a fresh grave beside it I said a quiet prayer over and did not ask after. The mountains loomed distant as we walked, and I remembered the howls we heard from that direction last time, and felt the same old dread settle over me again.

We found [[Leana]] by her wolf tracks, denned in a bramble of roots with a pack of direwolves who paid us no more mind than they would a passing stag. I knelt before her as I always do — she deserves that much, child-shaped or not — and asked for her guidance. She had little comfort to give. But she knew where to point us next: another [[Handmaidens of Seraphyne|handmaiden]] still lives, waiting to be found, and with no more weight in her voice than if she were naming the weather, she turned to [[Draven Throssenheiser|Draven]] and told him that [[Handmaidens of Seraphyne|handmaiden]] was his own blood — a sister he has never known he had. I watched his face and saw a man who has spent his whole life certain of exactly who he isn't, suddenly unsure of it.

She would say nothing further until we'd thrown sticks for her wolves, so we did, because refusing felt like refusing a child, however many centuries old that child may truly be. She gave us nothing more of substance after — only a promise she might visit us again, a giggle, and a farewell.

We returned to [[Theodore Silver|Theo]] with more questions than we'd left with. The flame is dead. The [[Handmaidens of Seraphyne|handmaiden]] we seek now, if [[Leana]] is to be believed, is [[Draven Throssenheiser|Draven]]'s own sister — kept from him his whole life by powers neither of us understands. I do not know what waits for us in [[Sanctuary]]. I only mean to be standing when we arrive.


## Session 30

*Session notes: [[Session 30]]*

I write this with my back against a cart wheel, in a camp of beaten men. [[Theodore Silver|Theo]] and [[Raouen Sanderfell|Raouen]] are not with us. I chose to leave them behind, and I will carry that choice until I can undo it. The road north to [[Sanctuary]] was thin. Thin fields, thin cattle, thin people watching us pass from their doorways with nothing left to fear because there is nothing left to take. War eats a land slowly and then all at once. I have seen it in [[Varanor]] and I see it here.

[[Hadron Ironwood|Hadron]] rode beside me most of the first day, looking at everything the way a child looks at snow. I asked where he hails from.
